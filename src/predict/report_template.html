Week 2 Score Board

Four models predict the exact final score of every Week 2 game. Each fits on every completed game through the date below, then predicts forward — the same code the backtest measured, so nothing here is produced by a separate path that could drift. The market columns are shown for comparison only and are never model inputs.

Reading a row

Scores are away–home, matching the matchup label. The combined model is the average of the other three; it is the one to read first.

Line is the closing spread and over/under. Implied score converts that same line into a score pair — (total ± spread) / 2 — so the market can be read on the same scale as the models.

The edge column

Combined margin minus the market's margin, in points. Teal means the model likes the home side more than the market does; rust means it likes the away side more.

A large edge means the model and the market disagree — not that the model is right.

Known bias, now corrected

Every model used to over-predict away scores by about +0.78 points, because training weighted the empty-stadium 2019–20 seasons — when home-field advantage was near zero — as heavily as last week.

Predictions now subtract the drift measured over the most recent season of training games. Across the three base models away bias fell to +0.25–+0.29, and margin bias from about −0.89 to −0.19.

This is not a betting tool. Against the closing spread these predictions hit 51.0% historically, where 52.4% is break-even at standard odds — a −2.7% return, and a rate indistinguishable from a coin flip at this sample size. The market also predicts margins more accurately than the models do (12.44 vs 12.87 points of typical error). Read this as a score forecast, not an edge.
