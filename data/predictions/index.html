NFL Score Ledger

Scores read away–home, rounded to one decimal. The models predict two point totals; margin and total fall out of those.

A winning record here is not an edge. Straight-up, the favourite wins roughly two NFL games in three, so a model picking 60% of winners has done nothing the closing line hadn't already told you. The number that would matter for betting is performance against the spread, and there this model sits at a coin flip. These grades measure whether it predicts scores, not whether it beats a market.

✓ and ✗ grade the winner only — the sign of the predicted margin against the sign of the real one. A model can be twenty points off and still have called the game, which is why the margin error sits next to the tick rather than replacing it.

The market column is reference only. Betting lines are never an input to any model — not as a feature, not as a prior, not as a sanity filter. It is the closing line, so a midweek prediction is being shown against a number that kept moving until kickoff.

Grading never edits a prediction. Each week is written to its own parquet the moment it is generated and is read back unchanged; the error columns are computed fresh from final scores at render time.

Weekly error is reported as MAE, the plain average of how far each prediction landed from the final score. The project's headline number elsewhere is RMSE, which squares the misses first — useful over 1,400 games, misleading over sixteen, where one blowout would swamp the other fifteen results.

Pre-injury-report marks a forecast written before that game's final injury report (Wednesday for Thursday games, Friday for Sunday, Saturday for Monday) was in the data. The model's injury signal was mostly missing, so treat it as provisional.
